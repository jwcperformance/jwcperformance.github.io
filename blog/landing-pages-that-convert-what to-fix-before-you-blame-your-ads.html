Landing Pages That Convert: What to Fix Before You Blame Your Ads

A great ad with a weak landing page still fails, and it usually gets misdiagnosed as an ads problem. Here's what to check on your landing page before you touch your campaigns.

Why a great ad still fails on a weak page

It's an easy mistake to make. The click happened, the cost per click looks reasonable, so if nobody converts, the ad must be the problem. Pause it, try a new one, tweak the bidding. But if the ad did its job and got the right person to click, the next few seconds on your site are what decide whether that click turns into anything.

A landing page problem gets misdiagnosed as an ads problem more often than people realise, mostly because it's easier to check ad performance in Google Ads than it is to sit down and honestly look at your own website through a stranger's eyes.

Message match

If your ad promises "same day boiler repair," the page someone lands on needs to say that, clearly, near the top, in similar words. If the ad leads with a specific offer and the landing page opens with a generic "welcome to our company" message, you've broken the connection the click was built on, and a chunk of visitors will simply leave, assuming they've landed somewhere else.

This applies to every ad in an account, not just one flagship page. If you're running several ad groups around different services, each one ideally sends traffic to a page that matches that specific promise, not a single homepage trying to speak to everyone at once.

The five second test

Give the page to someone who's never seen it, for five seconds, then ask three questions. What does this business do? Who is it for? What am I supposed to do next? If they can't answer all three, most visitors arriving from an ad won't either, and they won't spend longer figuring it out, they'll just leave.

This is usually a design and hierarchy problem rather than a content problem. It's not that the information isn't on the page, it's that it's competing with too much else for attention in those first few seconds.

Quick check: Screenshot your landing page and show it to someone unfamiliar with your business for five seconds. Ask them what you do and what they'd do next. Their answer tells you a lot.

Call to action clarity

One primary action, presented clearly, beats three competing options every time. If your page offers "Call now," "Get a quote," "Download our brochure" and "Learn more" all with equal visual weight, you haven't made a decision easier, you've made it harder. Visitors under time pressure, which is most people clicking a paid ad, tend to do nothing when faced with too many choices.

Pick the one action that matters most for that page and make it unmistakable. Everything else can still exist, just don't let it compete for the same attention.

Load speed and mobile experience

Most paid search traffic arrives on a phone, often from someone in the middle of doing something else. A page that takes several seconds to load, or that renders awkwardly on a small screen, loses a meaningful chunk of visitors before they've even seen what you offer.

This is worth checking directly rather than assuming it's fine. Load your own landing page on your own phone, on mobile data rather than wifi, and time how long it takes. If it feels slow to you on a good connection, it's costing you conversions on an average one.

Trust signals that matter

By the time someone's on your landing page, they're deciding whether to trust you with either their money or their contact details. Reviews, guarantees, clear pricing or a clear process all reduce the hesitation that stops someone converting. Vague reassurance like "trusted by many" does very little. A specific guarantee, a real review with a name attached, or a clear "here's exactly what happens next" section does a lot more.

The goal isn't to cram every trust signal you have onto the page, it's to place the two or three most relevant ones where they'll actually be seen, near the call to action itself rather than buried in a footer.

The bottom line

Before you touch your Google Ads campaigns because conversions look weak, spend fifteen minutes on the landing page itself. Check the message matches the ad, run the five second test, look for one clear action rather than several, check load speed on your own phone, and make sure the right trust signals are visible near the decision point. Often, the ads were doing exactly what they were supposed to, and the page was the part quietly letting the traffic go.
