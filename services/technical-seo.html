What's included

Four areas, all covered in one review.

Every review looks at the same four areas. What gets prioritised afterwards depends on what's actually wrong with your site.

Crawlability and indexation

Making sure Google can find and read your pages

Checking your sitemap, robots.txt and internal links for anything stopping Google from finding pages, plus a check for pages that are indexed but shouldn't be, or important pages that have been missed entirely.

Core Web Vitals and page speed

Faster pages, which Google rewards

Google measures how quickly your pages load, how soon someone can interact with them, and whether the layout jumps around while loading. Slow, unstable pages hold rankings back and lose visitors before they even see your content.

Structured data and schema

Helping Google understand what your content actually is

Structured data is a way of labelling your content so Google understands it properly, which can lead to richer search results like star ratings, FAQs or product details showing up directly in search.

Site structure and redirects

A clean structure Google can trust

Reviewing redirect chains, canonical tags and duplicate pages that can confuse Google about which version of a page to rank, and checking your site's structure makes sense for both visitors and search engines.

Signs you need this

How to tell if technical issues are holding you back.

These are the most common reasons a business ends up looking for technical SEO help.

01
Pages that should rank don't show up in Google at all
Usually a sign that Google either can't find the page, or has chosen not to index it. Both are fixable, but the cause needs identifying first.
02
Rankings dropped after a site migration or redesign
A common cause is broken redirects or lost internal links from the old site, which can quietly undo years of built up ranking signal.
03
Your site feels slow, especially on mobile
Slow loading pages affect Core Web Vitals scores and also cost you visitors who give up waiting before the page even loads.
04
Google Search Console is full of warnings you don't understand
Coverage errors, crawl issues and index warnings pile up in Search Console, but the tool rarely explains what to actually do about them.
05
You've invested in content or Google Ads, but organic traffic isn't moving
Good content and paid campaigns can only do so much if there are technical issues underneath stopping Google from properly crawling and ranking the site.
How it works

From first look to fixes going live.

A straightforward process, with a clear output at the end rather than an open ended engagement.

First

A full crawl of your site

Every page gets crawled and checked, using the same tools search engines use, to map out exactly what's there and what's wrong with it.

Then

A fix list ranked by impact

Issues get ranked by how much they're likely to be holding your site back, not just listed in the order a scanning tool found them.

Next

Fixes made directly, or handed to your developer

Some fixes can be made directly, others need a developer's help. Either way, you get clear instructions rather than jargon.

Finally

A recheck once fixes are live

Once changes go live, everything gets rechecked to confirm indexation has improved and Core Web Vitals are passing.

Not sure if it's technical SEO you need? A free Google Ads account audit often surfaces technical issues too, since landing page speed and quality directly affect ad performance. It's a useful starting point either way.

Common questions

Questions about technical SEO.

Answers to what comes up most often before a technical SEO review begins.

How much does technical SEO cost?

Pricing depends on the size of your site and the number of issues found, so it's a fixed fee agreed before any work starts rather than a set price for everyone. Get in touch for a quote specific to your site.

How long does a technical SEO review take?

A full review is usually delivered within 10 working days of getting access to your site and Search Console, though larger or more complex sites can take a little longer.

Do I need technical SEO if I already run Google Ads?

It still helps. Landing page speed and technical quality feed directly into Google's Quality Score for ads, so fixing technical issues can lower your cost per click as well as improving organic rankings.

Will you make the fixes, or just tell me what's wrong?

Both options are available. Some clients prefer fixes made directly, others prefer a clear list handed to their own developer. This gets agreed before work starts.

How is this different from a tool like Screaming Frog or Ahrefs?

Those tools are genuinely useful, and they're part of how a review gets done. The value on top is knowing which of the hundreds of issues a tool flags are actually worth fixing, and in what order, based on real experience of what moves rankings.
